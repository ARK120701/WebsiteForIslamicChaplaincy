The Founders

Our Team

Three Muslims. Three chaplaincy professionals. One shared commitment to growing the next generation of Muslim chaplains in America.

AK

Abdurrahman Khan

Chaplain Intern

North Shore University Hospital

Manhasset, New York

Abdurrahman Khan is an Islamic scholar, chaplain, and educator dedicated to advancing excellence in Muslim spiritual care. He memorized the Qur'an at Darul Uloom New York before pursuing advanced studies in the traditional Islamic sciences. He is a graduate of Darul Uloom New York Seminary and earned a Master's degree in Islamic Sciences from Jamia Binoria. He is currently engaged in advanced specialization (Iftāʼ) studies, training to become a Mufti. In addition to his religious scholarship, Abdurrahman holds a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science from CUNY Queens College, reflecting his commitment to integrating traditional scholarship with contemporary professional disciplines.

Abdurrahman serves as a Chaplain Intern at North Shore University Hospital, where he provides comprehensive spiritual care to patients, families, and healthcare professionals through Clinical Pastoral Education (CPE). His clinical ministry spans a wide range of care settings, including critical care, emergency medicine, and end-of-life care, where he accompanies individuals through moments of illness, loss, uncertainty, and healing. His approach to chaplaincy is rooted in the Islamic principles of compassion, mercy, human dignity, and attentive presence while serving people of all faiths and backgrounds.

Recognizing the growing need for qualified Muslim chaplains and greater representation within healthcare, Abdurrahman is committed to strengthening the profession through education, mentorship, and leadership. As Co-Founder of the National Muslim Chaplains Association (NMCA), he works to support aspiring and practicing Muslim chaplains by fostering professional development, promoting clinical excellence, and building a collaborative network dedicated to advancing the field of Muslim chaplaincy throughout North America.

ZW

Zuhaib Waqar

Chaplain Resident

North Shore University Hospital

Manhasset, New York

Zuhaib Waqar is an Islamic scholar, chaplain, and educator committed to advancing the profession of Muslim spiritual care through clinical excellence, scholarship, and leadership. He memorized the Qur'an at Flushing Muslim Center before pursuing advanced studies in the traditional Islamic sciences, graduating from Darus Salam Seminary in Lombard, Illinois. Alongside his religious studies, he is completing a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science from CUNY John Jay College, reflecting his commitment to integrating traditional scholarship with contemporary professional disciplines.

Zuhaib serves as a Chaplain Resident at North Shore University Hospital, where he is completing a year-long Clinical Pastoral Education (CPE) residency. In this role, he provides comprehensive spiritual care to patients, families, and healthcare professionals across a wide range of clinical settings, including critical care, emergency medicine, and palliative care. His ministry is grounded in the Islamic values of compassion, mercy, dignity, and presence, accompanying individuals through illness, crisis, grief, and end-of-life experiences with professionalism and empathy.

Through his clinical formation, Zuhaib has developed a particular interest in the integration of Islamic pastoral theology with contemporary healthcare, striving to ensure that Muslim patients receive spiritually and culturally responsive care while contributing meaningfully to interdisciplinary healthcare teams. He is passionate about expanding the role of Muslim chaplains within healthcare institutions and strengthening the profession through education, mentorship, and clinical training.

As Co-Founder of the National Muslim Chaplains Association (NMCA), Zuhaib is dedicated to supporting the development of current and future Muslim chaplains. He is committed to fostering professional excellence, expanding access to mentorship and educational resources, and building a collaborative network that advances the standards and visibility of Muslim chaplaincy throughout North America.

MI

Mohammed Ibraheem

Chaplain Resident

North Shore University Hospital

Manhasset, New York

Mohammed Ibraheem is an Islamic scholar, chaplain, and educator committed to advancing the field of Muslim spiritual care through clinical excellence, scholarship, and leadership. He memorized the Qur'an at a young age before pursuing advanced studies in the traditional Islamic sciences. He graduated from Darul Quran Was Sunnah Seminary and earned a Master's degree in Islamic Studies from Darul Uloom Karachi. In addition to his religious scholarship, Mohammed holds a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science, reflecting his commitment to integrating traditional Islamic learning with contemporary professional disciplines.

Mohammed serves as a Chaplain Resident at North Shore University Hospital, where he is completing a year-long Clinical Pastoral Education (CPE) residency while providing comprehensive spiritual care to patients, families, and healthcare professionals. His clinical ministry spans a broad range of hospital settings, accompanying individuals through illness, trauma, grief, and end-of-life care. His approach to chaplaincy is rooted in the Prophetic model of compassion, presence, and service, while drawing upon the clinical competencies developed through CPE to provide patient-centered, interfaith spiritual care.

Passionate about the future of Muslim chaplaincy, Mohammed plans to continue his Clinical Pastoral Education beyond residency and is preparing to serve as a Certified Educator Candidate (CEC) in Jacksonville, Florida. His professional interests include Islamic bioethics, healthcare ethics, end-of-life care, and the formation of future Muslim chaplains through high-quality clinical education.

As Co-Founder of the National Muslim Chaplains Association (NMCA), Mohammed is dedicated to strengthening the profession by promoting excellence in clinical pastoral care, expanding educational opportunities, and fostering a network of Muslim chaplains equipped to serve diverse communities across North America. He is committed to ensuring that Muslim chaplains are well-prepared to advocate for patients, contribute meaningfully to interdisciplinary healthcare teams, and uphold the highest standards of compassionate spiritual care.

Together

Three Paths, One Purpose

Though each of us came to chaplaincy through our own journey, we share a common conviction: that the Muslim community in America is ready to answer the call of professional spiritual care, and that with the right guidance, thousands of Muslims can enter this field and transform it.

We are not here as distant advisors — we are practitioners, in the rooms, doing the work every day. That lived experience is what we bring to this organization, and what we offer to everyone who joins us.
